Summarize this in 3 bullet points for a newsletter:

Coral bleaching occurs when water temperatures rise and corals expel the symbiotic algae living in their tissues, turning white. Bleached corals are not dead but are under stress and more vulnerable to disease. If temperatures return to normal quickly, corals can recover their algae. Prolonged heat, however, leads to widespread mortality, as seen in the 2016 and 2017 Great Barrier Reef events, which killed roughly half of the reef's shallow-water corals.
